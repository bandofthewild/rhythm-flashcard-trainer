Rhythm Flashcard Trainer

Fun, Practical Rhythm Drilling · Individual or Ensemble Sight‑Reading

Project rhythm flash cards for the whole ensemble to read and count together. Choose a meter, difficulty level, and groove; press Play for a count‑off and a metered pulse that sweeps the subdivisions in time. Cards range from a single measure to multi‑bar phrases across simple, compound, cut, and asymmetrical meters — a new card each turn, or hold one to work it.

Rhythm Flashcard Trainer — Quick Tour

Get Going

  • Pick a Time Signature and a Difficulty Level, then press Play.
  • Set the Tempo by dragging the slider, typing an exact BPM, or tapping it in — click Tap (or press the spacebar) in a steady four‑count at the speed you want. A couple of taps gives a rough guess; it locks onto an accurate tempo once you've tapped four times in a row.
  • You get a Get Ready count‑off, then Count It — the pulse sweeps the subdivisions while the class counts in "1 e & a."
  • Tap Combine to mix levels/complexities; use Rhythm Customization to pick exact note values.

Run the Drill

  • Cards: Auto flips each turn; Hold stays put — use Next ▶ / ◀ (or ← →) to advance on your cue.
  • Tap the card or press R to Reveal the counting; press F for Full‑Screen.
  • Steps adds a Clap It pass; Advanced Options hold card length, syllable reveal, "Hear It," and count‑off length.

Use Your Own Music

  • Your Own Music (next to Groove) → Load a Song… finds the track's tempo and downbeat, then plays it through the app so the drill locks to your music — no tapping, no drift.
  • If the downbeat lands wrong, press Tap Beat 1 (or the spacebar) right on beat 1; it comes in one bar later. ÷/× fix a half/double tempo reading.
  • Playing music from somewhere else (another device, a stream)? Set Groove to Off, then click Tap (or press the spacebar) four times in a row, right on the beat, to match it; tap a fresh four‑count anytime it drifts.

Your Own Music — How It Works

How It Works

  • Loading a song lets the app analyze the file itself and find its tempo and downbeat automatically — no tapping to get started.
  • The track then plays back through the app's own audio clock, and the drill rides that same clock — so the two can’t drift apart, however long the card runs.
  • Once it's loaded and analyzed, just press Play.

Best Practices

  • A clean recording with a clear, steady beat analyzes best; a rubato intro or heavy reverb can throw it off.
  • If the detected tempo is exactly half or double what it should be, use ÷ / × to fix it.
  • If the downbeat lands in the wrong place, press Tap Beat 1 (or spacebar) right on beat 1 of any bar — it snaps to the nearest beat and the drill starts one bar later, tempo untouched. ←/→ and ±20ms fine‑tune from there.

Playing From a Different Source

  • If the music is coming from somewhere the app can’t load — another device, a stream, a live accompanist — there’s no file to analyze, so it can’t lock on directly.
  • Instead, set Groove to Off, then click the Tap button (or press the spacebar) four times in a row, right on the beat, to match it by ear — the app locks onto a tempo once it has four taps.
  • Because the two clocks are separate, they will drift over time — tap a fresh four‑count now and then to re‑sync.
  • Loading the audio file here is always the more reliable choice when you can — that’s why it gets its own button.
